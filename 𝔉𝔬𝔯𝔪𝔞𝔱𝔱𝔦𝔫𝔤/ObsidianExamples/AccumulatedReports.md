# Accumulated reports make it easy to collect information from several notes into one.

Copy the following Markdown code into a new Obsidian note. You may want to use a fresh vault to make the process easier to see, then create your own based on what you find important and how you want to use the template.

For the sake of making the example easier to use, I will be using generic day names, but I recommend creating daily notes via a common naming scheme (such as YYYY/MM/DD). Once you have a set of naming schemes, you can turn this into a template that automatically creates a weekly review and all the component notes at once with just the click of a button.

Remember that section titles in Obsidian are collapsible. If you only want to review your People notes, you can click next to the Weekly Tasks and Financial Report to hide all of the references and focus only on the information you are interested in in that moment.

```
# Weekly Progress Report

## Weekly tasks
![[Sunday]]
![[Monday]]
![[Tuesday]]
![[Wednesday]]
![[Thursday]]
![[Friday]]
![[Saturday]]

## This week's financial report
![[Purchases (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]

## People
![[Work notes (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]
![[Family events (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]
![[Community events (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]

## Store Checklist
![[Foods (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]
![[Medicine (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]
![[Other Supplies (week of YYYY/MM/DD)]]
```

## Next, start filling in information

Once you create this note, you can then click on each of the links to access that note and fill each in with information. 

For each of the "Tasks", enter what you plan to do that day. Use checkboxes "- [ ] " so you can check things off once complete. I highly encourage this since it lets you see all the things you did at the end of the day, and that can give you the motivation to pursue more things. If all you see is what has not been done, it's easy to get discouraged or to start avoiding looking at the list.

For the "Financial Report", simply enter any items you purchased (or better yet, plan on but have not purchased) as they come up.
  * I recommend putting all items you are interested in in a shopping cart or list, then review it at least one week later. You will find that many items no longer seem to appeal to you.
  * Delaying purchasing until you have slept on the idea distances yourself from the emotional marketing tricks and gives you time to ask yourself if a better option exists.

For the People section, simply note down stuff that happens. Birthdays, yard sales, that story Bob told you after raking leaves.
  * The act of reviewing information after you have forgotten it prompts your brain to try and remember it better.

For the "Store Checklist", simply write down anything you find you are short on during the week. At the end of it, you can make a single shopping list for each category.
  * By making a single shopping list over a full week, you are more likely to add things to it that are easily forgotten.
    * If you have ever left the store and come home only to realize that you forgot dish soap or something else equally mundane, you know how easy and annoying this is.
  * By making a comprehensive list, you are less likely to feel inclined to browse for things you don't need.

## Finally, review it and see what you notice about yourself and what you want to do next.

***A note does nothing if it's not used.***  

Reviewing a list of notes at the end of the week gives you a chance to remember and process the information. You can make notes at any time, then review all of them when you have the time to sit down and really plan ahead.

If you find yourself adding something specific regularly (ex: a monthly medication, short shelf life foods like milk, meetings you need to review later), consider putting those entries in "common" sections of your notes.  
  * For example, I love sweet and sour chicken. I have plenty of spices, and I buy and freeze chicken whenever convenient, but the bell peppers don't last as long. The bell peppers can therefor be put in my "Foods" list automatically every week and I can decide to add them to my shopping list whenever I feel like I want sweet and sour chicken. I then use them each time I make a batch, so I know I always needs to buy more if I plan to cook some the next week.

One additional benefit of keeping notes about this stuff regularly is that you can review it over longer time scales as well.  
  * Look at food expenses and see if potato chips are popping up more often than vegetable.  
  * Take note of projects that you keep putting off and choose to either set a date or remove the project entirely. Unfinished jobs cause stress and eat away at attention.  
  * Take a look at what you buy regularly, and look for bulk versions or DIY options instead to save money.  
  * Use the people notes to track things like birthdays and anniversaries, then put them on a calendar so you know in advance next year.
